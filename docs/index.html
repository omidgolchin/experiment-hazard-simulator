How the verdict is computed model, simulation, sources

Model. Each task has a difficulty (normal on the log-odds scale; "similar", "mixed" and "very mixed" are standard deviations of 0.5, 1 and 2), and each attempt adds noise (0.5). Version B's improvement is set so that the average success rate rises by the points you entered. Your current success rate sets the baseline.

Analysis. With the same tasks for both versions: a paired t-test on per-task differences in success rate, because the task, not the attempt, is the unit that varies. With separate task sets: a Welch t-test on per-task success rates.

Chance to detect is the share of 2,000 simulated evals of the clean design (no risky answers) that find B significantly better at the 5% level. Fake wins is the share of simulated evals with no real difference that find a significant one, with your checklist answers applied; a sound design sits near 5%. Tasks for 80% comes from the standard formula n = ((z0.975 + z0.8) · sd / gain)², with sd the simulated spread of per-task differences, and the chart confirms it by simulation.

The checklist is the hazard list of the agent-experiment-methodology skill (agent-hazards.md), each with the check that catches it. Background: Hurlbert (1984) on pseudo-replication; Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn (2011) on selective reporting; Panickssery, Bowman & Feng (2024) on LLM judges favoring their own outputs.

The simulation studies behind this tool (how each hazard's false-alarm rate grows with its strength) are in Research notes. All data on this page are simulated.

How the results are analyzed paired by task, bootstrap

Each task's success rate is computed for each version, and the analysis runs on the per-task differences: a paired t-interval and, as a check that does not assume normality, a percentile bootstrap that resamples tasks (2,000 resamples). Tasks that ran on only one version are left out and listed.

The "attempts as independent" interval is what you get by pooling all attempts into two success rates. Attempts on the same task are correlated, so that interval is usually too narrow when the versions ran on different tasks; with shared tasks it can also be too wide, because it ignores the pairing. Either way it answers the wrong question.
